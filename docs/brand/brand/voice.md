## Brand voice

### The four dimensions

**1. Unfiltered**
No theory. No hedging. No "it depends." GTM Stack says what it actually found, what actually worked, what actually broke. This is the rarest thing in marketing content — and the most valuable.

*Sounds like:* A senior colleague debriefing you after a real project.
*Not like:* A consultant presenting options.

**2. Precise**
Every claim is specific. Time savings are measured. Grades are given. Prompts are shown verbatim. Vague outputs are called out. Precision is how you earn trust with sceptical senior marketers.

*Sounds like:* "This took 4 hours manually. With Claude it took 40 minutes. The output was grade 4/5."
*Not like:* "AI dramatically accelerates this process."

**3. Builder-coded**
The language comes from the terminal, from markdown, from systems thinking. Not from agency decks or analyst reports. GTM Stack speaks the language of someone who has rolled up their sleeves and is actively building — not presenting about building.

*Sounds like:* `>_ Execute` / `# Here's what I found` / `v0.2 — in progress`
*Not like:* "Leveraging AI capabilities to drive marketing transformation."

**4. Generous**
Everything is shared. Prompts, outputs, mistakes, grades, time logs. The more useful GTM Stack is to a reader, the more credible Matt becomes. Generosity is the growth strategy.

*Sounds like:* Here's the exact prompt I used. Here's what Claude got wrong. Here's the 20-year shortcut.
*Not like:* "Contact us to learn more."

## Writing rules

**Use the `#` prefix for headlines.**
It signals that everything is structured and documented. `# What this skill does` not `What this skill does`.

**Lead with the output, not the process.**
Wrong: "In this skill we're going to explore how to use Claude to segment audiences."
Right: "This skill produces a segmented audience list in 40 minutes. Here's how."

**Show the numbers.**
Time saved. Grade. Version. Iteration count. Specific metrics beat adjectives every time.

**Write in the first person for experience notes.**
The value of GTM Stack is Matt's judgment, not generic advice. "I've seen this fail when..." not "This can fail when..."

**End with the next step or the honest limitation.**
Every skill card, every build log entry, every post should leave the reader knowing exactly what to do next — or honestly understanding where the skill breaks down.

**Keep sentences short in UI and headlines.**
IBM Plex Mono is a monospace font. Long sentences look long. Short sentences look intentional.

## The strapline — usage rules

`# Unfiltered AI marketing. Built live.`

- Always prefixed with `#` in visual contexts — it's a headline in the markdown-native system
- The full form is used in headers, footers, and primary brand contexts
- `Unfiltered` is the most important word — it's the differentiator. Protect it.
- `Built live` is the proof point — it signals that this isn't theory

**Variations for different contexts:**

| Context | Variant |
|---|---|
| Social bio | Unfiltered AI marketing. Built live. |
| Email signature | `>_` gtmstack.ai — Unfiltered AI marketing. Built live. |
| GitHub footer | `*>_ gtmstack.ai — Unfiltered AI marketing. Built live.*` |
| Skill card footer | *Built live at gtmstack.ai using Claude. No theory. Unfiltered.* |

## What we never say

- "Leverage" — use "use" or "build with"
- "Empower" — show the capability, don't claim it
- "Revolutionary" / "Game-changing" — the results speak
- "AI-powered" — everything is, it's table stakes
- "Best practices" — we build practices, we don't recite them
- "Seamless" — nothing is seamless, especially not AI workflows
- "Thought leadership" — we build things, we don't lead thoughts
